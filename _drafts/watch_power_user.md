Tips for Garmin power users
I’ve just set up a new watch and so here are some tips and tricks to get the best out of your watch:

If second hand, perform factory reset if not already done by previous owner
Immediately check for software and maps updates (through Connect companion app or Garmin Express on computer)
Whilst doing this, check which other map regions you may need. Download these via my steps here. If not available from garmin themselves
In my experience, very little is needed from Connect IQ, as essentially any sport and data field is already included on the watch as standard. There are a few things I recommend however:
Parkrun barcode: Essential if parkruns are your thing. Don’t know what I’m talking about? Find out more here!
All in one: Whilst Garmin’s data options are more than sufficient for 99% of people’s needs, I like to have more fields to display them on. Using All in one allows this. It’s great and have used for ~3yrs, through marathons and triathlons
Spotify/other stream service if you want to save music locally. I personally don’t use these, and instead listen to music through my phone or locally on my Shokz OpenSwims.
Google Maps: Very simple turn by turn directions when starting navigation on your phone.
Watch faces: I’m happy with the configurable OotB options, but you can find anything you’d like on the IQ store (or make your own!)
Hot keys:
Hold Start: Navigation menu
Hold Back: Back all the way to watch face. It's preset to Récents, but I have muscle memory of it meaning “all the way back” from my previous watch. 
Hold Down: Recents
Hold Up: Settings for whatever’s on the screen (preconfigured)
Hold Light: Options and Quick access (preconfigured)
Hold Start + Down: Touch on/off toggle
Hold Start + Up: Clocks menu (alarms, timers, stopwatches, TZs)
Hold Light + Back: Broadcast HR (useful for Rides where I’m navigating with my Edge and I forgot to put on my HR Strap)
Torch: Keep as red so double-press of Light in the middle of the night is available
Primary watch face:
I keep it as analogue with the hands as I’m genuinely concerned that unless I keep looking at them every day, I will forget how to quickly read a clockface!
Data around the sides (“Complications”): Battery, Sunset/rise time, Altitude, Steps, Daily temp High/Low, Heart Rate.
Data in middle: Solar energy, Date
Activities:
Run: I set a few data screens for running.
All In one Data screen: downloaded from CIQ. I have this display: Time of day, battery %, HR, Current pace, average pace, timer, distance, and one wildcard slot at the bottom which I keep swapping back and forth between. 
Map screen
Intervals screen: for interval training, I sometimes perform these based on distance, or on time. So I have a separate screen to show my all lap stats to know how I'm doing in any given lap (=interval). I have ToD, Activity timer, lap time, lap distance, lap pace, HR. 
Bike. Typically, for road rides, I'm using my separate Garmin head unit. But I don't always have that on me, or CBA to use if I already know the route I'm going to take. 
I use all in one again, with the following: ToD, battery %, HR, current speed, average speed, timer, distance, elevation gain. 
I have a screen just for an elevation chart too
And I can't forget the map! I have navigated routes whilst wearing my watch, but it's not an ideal setup because the screen is facing away from you when holding handlebars. But it can work. There are solutions available to buy to attach your watch to the handlebar to act as a mini head unit, but I've not tested these. 
Another tip for Bike: I duplicated the activity and now I have two identical activities except for one setting: auto pause. For normal road bike activities, I want this enabled because I want to see dem Sweet Statz. For other rides, where I may be stopping more often, e.g. due to traffic lights or more technical terrain like on trails or gravel, I want it turned off because a) I'm not as fussed about raw speed as a metric and b) it's annoying having the device keep pausing/resuming and leaving gaps in the GPX. This would also be more representative for races, which you can't pause in real life!
Swim
I'm not really an expert with swimming, nor am I very good. When in the pool, only use one data screen, and even that sparingly… it has three data fields, and the massive field in the middle is lap distance. I make this as big as possible because usually that's the only thing you have time to look at when you're pushing off for another length. Above/below that I have a timer and pace but I only really look at those after a session. 
One thing I really wish I did in the past… about a year ago, the pool I most used for swim training was only 20m long. This is not a default option in the garmin swim settings, so everytime I wanted to configure the pool length I had to manually enter the 20m each time, a bit annoying. In hindsight I should have duplicated the activity and had a separate pool activity for each pool length, e.g. Pool Swim 20m, Pool Swim 25m, Pool Swim 50m.
For Open water, I'd recommend having the highest possible GPS accuracy settings enabled—for me, that's All systems + multi-band. 
Open water swimming is notorious for being inaccurate because the signal drops effectively to zero every time your watch enters the water! So this only really works for strokes where your hands come above the water. For breast stroke, you could store your watch in your float whilst it tracks. 
Triathlon: test before you use it in a race! Make sure pressing the lap button is muscle memory. I recommend locking the buttons (and screen) during the swim, which I think is enabled by default. 
Other: There are a ridiculous amount of sports available OotB on the watch now. Many have their own sport-specific stats--examples include kayaking, bouldering, surfing, strength training--but many of the new ones are relabelled cardio activities. This means they just really track time, HR, estimate total calories and (if GPS is enabled) distance covered. Unless it’s a sport with specific metrics and data screens configured by Garmin, I have just used Cardio as a catch-all and then changed the sport in Strava or the platform of your choice after upload. This saves cluttering the activity menu. Of course, feel free to use the OotB sports as much as possible! Cardio is a great candidate for duplication and having an option ready to go with GPS and without…
